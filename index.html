Metabolic syndrome — Indian / South Asian men context

Educational synthesis with public research links — not a personal diagnosis. BMI thresholds and risk calculators built on Western cohorts under-estimate South Asian risk.

Why your build matters

  • Age ~40, height ~183 cm (6'0"), weight 104 kg, BMI ~31 — obese by WHO, but South Asians often develop diabetes, CAD, and MetS at lower BMI than Europeans because of higher visceral fat and lower muscle mass (“thin-fat” / Y-Y paradox).
  • Consensus MetS waist cut-offs for South Asian men are often ≥90 cm (not 102 cm). Your report had no waist — measure and track.
  • MetS prevalence in South Asia is commonly cited around ~25–35% in mid-life adults depending on definition/setting; risk rises sharply with age and central obesity.

Prediabetes — reversible window

HbA1c 6.0% is ADA prediabetes (5.7–6.4). Indian trials show lifestyle can cut diabetes incidence:

  • IDPP-1 (Asian Indians with IGT): lifestyle ~28.5% relative risk reduction over ~3 years.
  • D-CLIP (Chennai): culturally tailored lifestyle ± stepwise metformin → ~32% RRR.
  • CDC-style programs: ~5–7% weight loss + 150+ min/week brisk activity roughly halves progression risk in many cohorts.

Your fasting glucose is still normal — this is the best time to reverse course with cardio return + diet + further weight loss.
