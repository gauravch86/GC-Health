## 2. What “inferior infarct, probably old” means in plain English

The computer thinks the **bottom wall** of the left ventricle (fed mainly by the right coronary artery) shows an electrical pattern that **can** mean scar from a past heart attack — often **Q waves** in the inferior leads (**II, III, aVF**).

“Probably old” = it does **not** look like a heart attack happening *right now* (no acute STEMI pattern). It is guessing at **old scar**.

Important: an ECG is a **pattern recognition** tool. Patterns can come from:
1. True old heart-attack scar  
2. **Normal variant** (especially lead **III**)  
3. **Lead placement** a bit off  
4. Body habitus / heart position / axis  
5. Athlete remodeling / localized hypertrophy  
6. Other rare causes  

The machine does not know you can run 15 km. It only sees voltages.

## 3. Why your fitness does *not* prove the ECG is wrong — and why it also does *not* prove you had a heart attack

### What your running *does* prove
- Your heart can raise output for a long time (150–160 bpm sustained, spikes ~180).
- You are **not** in heart-failure territory or severe limiting ischemia at those loads (symptoms absent).
- Low resting HR (**54** in 2025) is classic **athlete / endurance** physiology — Bluecrest even says that.

### What your running does *not* prove
- Silent or small **old** scars can exist in people who still exercise hard (especially if the scar is small or the other walls compensate).
- Risk factors still matter: **BP high 2–3 years**, weight ~110 kg, **low HDL**, **smoking since 2021**, prior liver fat pattern.
- So fitness = **reassuring context**, not a cancel button on the ECG.

### What the science says about machine “old inferior MI” reads
Studies comparing computer “old inferior MI” calls to imaging find a **high false-positive rate**. In one series, computer diagnosis of old inferior MI had only about **~53% positive predictive value** vs imaging — roughly **half** of automated “old inferior MI” labels were **not** confirmed as infarct when scanned. Accuracy rose when a human required wider Q waves **and** ST/T changes.

Athlete ECG guidelines (International Criteria) are stricter about calling Q waves “pathological”:
- Need Q/R ≥ 0.25 **or** Q ≥ 40 ms in **two contiguous leads**
- **Lead III alone is often excluded** as unreliable  
Isolated or borderline inferior Q waves are a **common pitfall** and a major source of false alarms in active people.

**Bottom line:** Being a strong runner makes a *large* damaging old infarct less likely as a *life-story*, but the most common explanation for this report pattern in someone like you is still: **algorithm over-call ± lead III/aVF Q waves ± technique** — which is exactly why the printout says **“Please correlate clinically.”**

## 4. Why 2025 said “Q waves normal” and 2026 said “infarct”

Possible reconciliations (doctor decides which):
1. **Different machines / criteria** (Bluecrest limb-lead screen vs full 12-lead Tricog).
2. **Electrode placement** differed (inferior leads are sensitive to that).
3. **Borderline Q morphology** — one system ignores, one over-calls.
4. Less likely: a **real event** between Sep 2025 and Sep 2026 you did not notice (“silent”). Possible but not the default assumption without symptoms, enzymes, or imaging.
5. Weight/axis/BP changes slightly altering voltages.

Your symptom history (no weakness, strong endurance) argues against a big recent infarct — but **does not replace echo**.

## 6. What you should do (calm, specific)

1. **See a cardiologist** — take **both** ECGs (2025 + 2026) and this note. Ask: “Is this pathological Q waves by athlete criteria, or a false positive?”
2. Typical next step if unclear: **echocardiogram** (wall motion / scar hint). Sometimes **stress test** or **CT angio / MRI** if risk factors + ECG remain uncertain.
3. **Do not stop all activity** out of panic — but get **clearance before hard interval / max efforts** if the cardiologist wants that. Steady Zone-2 running you’ve always done is usually discussed case-by-case.
4. Keep fixing **BP, weight, smoking, lipids** — those matter for *future* heart risk regardless of whether 2026 was a false alarm.
